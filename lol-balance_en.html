Analytical Balance Assistant (League of Legends)

MBA Capstone Project - Ibmec BH [cite: 79]

1. The Business Challenge

The longevity and financial success of modern Games as a Service (GaaS) heavily depend on robust player retention and long-term engagement[cite: 87]. Within competitive ecosystems like League of Legends, structural imbalances degrade match integrity, making gameplay repetitive and predictable[cite: 89, 94]. This drop in engagement leads to measurable business impacts, such as reduced revenue from cosmetic sales and skins [cite: 95], alongside brand reputation risks generated by community dissatisfaction[cite: 96]. This project tackles competitive imbalance by delivering a data-driven "balance assistant" that uncovers underlying causal factors, moving beyond shallow win-rate metrics[cite: 90, 97].

2. Data Pipeline and Feature Engineering

High-quality, granular match data was mined directly through the Riot Games API[cite: 100, 109]. To ensure the data reflected optimal strategic execution and true meta conditions, the extraction script targeted high-tier competitive ranks (Challenger, Grandmaster, and Master) on the Korean (KR) server[cite: 102, 103].

The semi-structured JSON payloads were parsed and flattened into clean, tabular Pandas DataFrames, with each row modeling an individual player's performance[cite: 109, 112]. Using Riot's Data Dragon service, numeric item IDs were mapped to human-readable text labels[cite: 113]. Furthermore, a custom feature called "game_phase" was engineered to slice matches into distinct early, mid, and late-game stages, unlocking more granular vectors for performance evaluation[cite: 115].

3. Predictive Machine Learning Modeling

At the center of this diagnostic tool is a predictive Random Forest machine learning classifier trained to evaluate match outcomes based on contextual gameplay features[cite: 118, 119]. Rather than tracking static variables, the features were carefully selected to capture structural synergies: combining specific champions with their chosen Keystone Runes (e.g., Lee Sin + Conqueror) [cite: 122], incorporating performance metrics like gold per minute and total damage dealt [cite: 121], and transforming items into binary variables to isolate their individual correlation with a match outcome[cite: 123]. The resulting model achieved a robust classification accuracy of 0.87 when predicting victories or defeats[cite: 124].

4. Actionable Design Insights

Evaluating the model’s Feature Importance attributes revealed critical design levers that can actively guide development teams during live balancing loops[cite: 128, 134]:

  • Itemization Dominance: Item variables demonstrated an overwhelming statistical importance on match success, outclassing individual champion or rune variables[cite: 129]. Core choices like Ionian Boots of Lucidity, Farsight Alteration, and Plated Steelcaps showed extremely high feature importance values, proving that item tuning is a much more powerful design lever for meta shifts than micro-adjusting base champion statistics[cite: 130].
  • Systemic Synergy Balance: The immense weight placed on champion-to-rune combinations suggests that evaluating a character in isolation is an inefficient balancing paradigm[cite: 131, 132]. Designers must balance the interactions across systems, targeting the synergy vectors where competitive toxicity or over-performance genuinely originates[cite: 132, 138].
